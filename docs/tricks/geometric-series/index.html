Geometric series

  • Middle school: problems 61–80
  • High school: problems 41–60

Use it when: A sum where each term is a fixed fraction of the last, either forever or up to a last term.

How to do it

  1. Ratio r= second term ÷ first term.
  2. If ∣r∣<1⁠, the sum is 1−rfirst​⁠.
  3. A sum that stops at a last term ℓ⁠: 1−ra−rℓ​⁠. With r=21​ that's just 2a−ℓ⁠.

Worked example

8−4+2+⋯=
  1. Ratio r=−4÷8=−21​⁠.
  2. Sum = first ÷(1−r)=8÷121​= 531​
Why it worksS=a+rS⁠, so S(1−r)=a⁠.
Watch outAlternating signs mean r is negative, so 1−r is bigger than 1.

Try these

  1. 8+24+72+216=
    Show the answer

    320

    1. Each term is 3 times the one before. For a growing geometric sum, S=r−1rℓ−a​⁠.
    2. (3×216−8)÷2= 320
  2. 2+10+50+250+1250=
    Show the answer

    1562

    1. Each term is 5 times the one before. For a growing geometric sum, S=r−1rℓ−a​⁠.
    2. (5×1250−2)÷4= 1562
  3. 27+9+3+⋯=
    Show the answer

    4021​ or 281​ or 40.5

    1. Ratio r=9÷27=31​⁠.
    2. Sum = first ÷(1−r)=27÷32​= 4021​
  4. 39+13+431​+194​+⋯+72913​=
    Show the answer

    58729358​ or 72942640​

    1. For a finite geometric sum, S=1−ra−rℓ​⁠, with first term a, ratio r and last term ℓ.
    2. (39−(31​⁠)(72913​))÷(1−31​)= 58729358​
