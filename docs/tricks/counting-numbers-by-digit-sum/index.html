Counting numbers by digit sum

  • High school: problems 41–60

Use it when: "The sum of the digits of a 3-digit number is 7. How many such numbers exist?"

How to do it

  1. For a digit sum s from 1 to 9, the count is 2s(s+1)​⁠. Sum 7: 27⋅8​=28⁠.
  2. For 10 to 18, start with 2s(s+1)​ and take away the ways a digit would have to be 10 or more: (2s−8​) for the first digit and (2s−9​) for each of the other two. Sum 13: 91−10−2⋅6=69⁠.
  3. Above 18, use 28−s instead: sum 20 has the same count as sum 8, which is 36.
  4. Worth memorizing: 1, 3, 6, 10, 15, 21, 28, 36, 45 for sums 1 to 9, then 54, 61, 66, 69, 70 for 10 to 14.

Worked example

The sum of the digits of a 3-digit number is 8. How many such numbers exist?
  1. Up to 9 there's no carrying to worry about: the count is 2s(s+1)​⁠.
  2. 8×9÷2= 36
Why it worksThe first digit is at least 1. Take 1 off it and you're splitting s−1 among three digits, which is (2s+1​) ways. The subtractions remove the splits that would need a digit bigger than 9.
Watch outThe first digit can't be 0. That's why sum 1 has only one number, 100.

Try these

  1. The sum of the digits of a 3-digit number is 6. How many such numbers exist?
    Show the answer

    21

    1. Up to 9 there's no carrying to worry about: the count is 2s(s+1)​⁠.
    2. 6×7÷2= 21
  2. The sum of the digits of a 3-digit number is 13. How many such numbers exist?
    Show the answer

    69

    1. Start with 2s(s+1)​=91, then remove the ways a digit would have to be 10 or more: 10 for the first digit and 6 each for the other two.
    2. 91−10−12= 69
  3. The sum of the digits of a 3-digit number is 10. How many such numbers exist?
    Show the answer

    54

    1. Start with 2s(s+1)​=55, then remove the ways a digit would have to be 10 or more: 1 for the first digit and 0 each for the other two.
    2. 55−1−0= 54
  4. The sum of the digits of a 3-digit number is 19. How many such numbers exist?
    Show the answer

    45

    1. Swap each of the last two digits for 9 minus itself, and the first digit for 10 minus itself. The digit sum becomes 28−19=9, so the count is the same as for sum 9.
    2. 9×10÷2= 45
