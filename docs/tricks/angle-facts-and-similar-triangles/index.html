Angle facts and similar triangles

  • Middle school: problems 21–40
  • High school: problems 21–40

Use it when: Missing angles, exterior angles, inscribed angles, complements and similar figures.

How to do it

  1. Triangle angles add to 180°. An exterior angle equals the two far interior angles added together.
  2. Complements add to 90°, supplements to 180°. An inscribed angle is half its arc.
  3. Similar figures: every length scales by the same factor.
  4. Scale factor k⁠: perimeters multiply by k⁠, areas by k2⁠, volumes by k3⁠.
  5. Triangle angles in arithmetic progression: the middle one is 60°. Quadrilateral: the smallest and largest add to 180°, and so do the other two.
  6. Parallelograms and trapezoids: the two angles along one side add to 180°.

Worked example

A 3" by 4" picture is enlarged to 15" by 20". Its perimeter is multiplied by
  1. Every length is multiplied by 5.
  2. Perimeter is a length: 5
Why it worksA handful of theorems cover nearly all of these.
Watch outIn an isosceles triangle the two base angles match, not the vertex angle and a base angle.
