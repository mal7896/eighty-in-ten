Primes

  • Middle school: problems 1–20
  • High school: problems 1–20

Use it when: Largest prime below a number, or counting and adding primes in a range.

How to do it

  1. Know the primes through 100 by heart.
  2. To test a number, check divisibility by primes up to its square root.

Worked example

The largest prime number less than 66 is
  1. Walk down from 65, skipping evens and multiples of 3, 5, 7.
  2. 61 is prime.
Why it worksA composite number always has a factor no bigger than its square root.
Watch out1 isn't prime. Neither is 91=7⋅13⁠.

Try these

  1. The largest prime number less than 62 is
    Show the answer

    61

    1. Walk down from 61, skipping evens and multiples of 3, 5, 7.
    2. 61 is prime.
  2. How many primes are between 50 and 60?
    Show the answer

    2

    1. They are 53, 59.
    2. Count: 2
  3. How many primes are between 30 and 50?
    Show the answer

    5

    1. They are 31, 37, 41, 43, 47.
    2. Count: 5
  4. The sum of the primes between 40 and 50 is
    Show the answer

    131

    1. Primes: 41, 43, 47.
    2. Sum: 131
