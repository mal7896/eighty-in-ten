Right triangles and triples

  • Middle school: problems 21–40
  • High school: problems 41–60

Use it when: Finding a side, the area, or the altitude to the hypotenuse.

How to do it

  1. Know the triples: 3–4–5, 5–12–13, 8–15–17, 7–24–25, 20–21–29, 9–40–41, 12–35–37, 11–60–61, and their multiples.
  2. Altitude to the hypotenuse: hypotenuseleg×leg​⁠.
  3. 45°–45°–90°: each leg is the hypotenuse ÷2​⁠. 30°–60°–90°: short leg s⁠, long leg s3​⁠, hypotenuse 2s⁠.

Worked example

A right triangle has legs 9 and 12. Its hypotenuse is
  1. Spot the triple: 3–4–5 scaled by 3.
  2. Hypotenuse 15
Why it worksThe area is half of leg × leg, and also half of hypotenuse × altitude.
Watch outPull out a common factor first: 24–45–51 is 3× (8–15–17).

Try these

  1. An isosceles right triangle has sides x,x and 32​⁠. x=
    Show the answer

    4

    1. x2+x2=32, so x2=16⁠.
    2. x= 4
  2. A 30°–60°–90° triangle has a longer leg of 73​⁠. The sum of the lengths of the other two sides is
    Show the answer

    21

    1. The longer leg is 3​ times the short leg, so the short leg is 7. The hypotenuse is twice that, 14.
    2. 7+14= 21
  3. A right triangle has hypotenuse 25 and one leg 7. The other leg is
    Show the answer

    24

    1. Spot the triple: 7–24–25.
    2. Other leg 24
  4. The shortest side of a 30°–60°–90° triangle is 2' 11". The longest side is inches
    Show the answer

    70

    1. 2′11"=35 inches. The hypotenuse is twice the short side.
    2. 2×35= 70 inches
