Whole-number solutions

  • Middle school: problems 61–80

Use it when: "x2+y2=85 with x>y>2…" and "How many nonnegative pairs solve 3x+5y=90⁠?"

How to do it

  1. Sum of two squares: go down the squares below N and check whether what's left is a square. 85−49=36⁠, so 7 and 6.
  2. ax+by=N⁠: find the first y that makes N−by a multiple of a⁠, then step y up by a until by passes N⁠.
  3. 3x+5y=90⁠: y must be a multiple of 3 from 0 to 18, so 7 pairs.

Worked example

If x and y are positive integers with x2+y2=74 and x>y⁠, then x+y=
  1. Take squares below 74 and look for a partner square: 74−49=25⁠.
  2. x=7⁠, y=5⁠: 12
Why it worksOnce you have one solution, adding a to y and taking b from x keeps the total the same.
Watch outRead the conditions. "Positive" leaves out 0; "nonnegative" lets it in.

  2. How many ordered pairs of nonnegative integers (x,y) solve 6x+7y=164⁠?
    Show the answer

    4

    1. y has to make 164−7y a multiple of 6. The first is y=2⁠, then every 6 after that.
    2. y=2,8,…,20⁠: 4
