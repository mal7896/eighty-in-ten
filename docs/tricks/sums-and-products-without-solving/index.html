Sums and products without solving

  • Middle school: problems 21–40
  • High school: problems 21–40

Use it when: Given x+y (or x−y⁠) and xy⁠, find x2+y2⁠, x3±y3 or ∣x+y∣⁠.

How to do it

  1. x2+y2=(x+y)2−2xy⁠.
  2. (x+y)2=(x−y)2+4xy⁠, so you can switch between a sum and a difference.
  3. x3+y3=(x+y)3−3xy(x+y)⁠.
  4. x3−y3=(x−y)3+3xy(x−y)⁠.

Worked example

If pq=−126 and p−q=−23⁠, then ∣p+q∣=
  1. (p+q)2=(p−q)2+4pq=529+−504=25⁠.
  2. ∣p+q∣= 5
Why it worksExpand the right side and the extra terms cancel. You never need x and y themselves, which helps, because they usually aren't whole numbers.
Watch outWatch the sign of xy⁠. With xy=−4⁠, the −3xy(x+y) term becomes a plus.

Try these

  1. Two positive numbers have a difference of 2 and a product of 48. Their sum is
    Show the answer

    14

    1. (sum)2=(difference)2+4× product =4+192=196⁠.
    2. Sum = 14
  2. If xy=3 and x+y=5, then x3+y3=
    Show the answer

    80

    1. x3+y3=(x+y)3−3xy(x+y)⁠.
    2. 53−3(3)(5)=125−45= 80
  3. Two positive numbers have a difference of 6 and a product of 247. Their sum is
    Show the answer

    32

    1. (sum)2=(difference)2+4× product =36+988=1024⁠.
    2. Sum = 32
  4. If xy=−4 and x−y=11, then x3−y3=
    Show the answer

    1199

    1. x3−y3=(x−y)3+3xy(x−y)⁠.
    2. 113+3(−4)(11)=1331+(−132)= 1199
