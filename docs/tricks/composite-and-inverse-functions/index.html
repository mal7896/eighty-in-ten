Composite and inverse functions

  • Middle school: problems 61–80
  • High school: problems 61–80

Use it when: f(g(3))⁠, f−1(11)⁠, or a domain.

How to do it

  1. Composite: work from the inside out.
  2. Inverse: f−1(y) is the x that makes f(x)=y⁠. Solve for it; skip finding the formula.
  3. Domain of a square root: set the inside ≥0⁠.

Worked example

f(x)=3x−3⁠. If f−1 is the inverse of f⁠, then f−1(3)=
  1. f−1(3) is the x with f(x)=3⁠.
  2. 3x−3=3, so x= 2
Why it worksAn inverse undoes the function.
Watch outf(g(x)) and g(f(x)) are usually different.
