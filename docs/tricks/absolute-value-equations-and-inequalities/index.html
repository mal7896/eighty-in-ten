Absolute value equations and inequalities

  • Middle school: problems 41–60

Use it when: Sums of solutions of ∣ax+b∣=c⁠, and sums or counts of integer solutions of ∣ax+b∣≤c⁠.

How to do it

  1. ∣ax+b∣=c⁠: the two solutions sit evenly around −ab​⁠, so they add to −a2b​⁠. The right side doesn't matter.
  2. For one solution, solve ax+b=c and ax+b=−c separately. The gap between them is a2c​⁠.
  3. ∣x−h∣≤k⁠: the integers from h−k to h+k⁠. There are 2k+1 of them and they add to (2k+1)h⁠.
  4. ∣ax+b∣≤c⁠: solve −c≤ax+b≤c⁠, find the integer ends, then sum = count × average of the ends.
  5. Given the solutions of ∣x−c∣=d⁠: c is their midpoint and d is half their distance.

Worked example

The larger solution of ∣x+13∣=8 is
  1. x+13=8 gives x=−5⁠; x+13=−8 gives x=−21⁠.
  2. The larger one: −5
Why it works∣x−h∣ is the distance from x to h⁠, so the solutions are balanced around h⁠.
Watch outWith < the ends drop out. When −ab​ isn't a whole number, the integer solutions aren't centered on it, so list the ends.

Try these

  1. The sum of the integral solutions of ∣x−7∣<5 is
    Show the answer

    63

    1. −5<x−7<5⁠, so x runs from 2 to 12, ends not included: integers 3 through 11.
    2. 9 integers centered on 7: sum =9×7= 63
  2. How many integers are solutions of ∣5x−2∣≤25⁠?
    Show the answer

    10

    1. −25≤5x−2≤25⁠, so x runs from −453​ to 552​⁠: integers −4 through 5.
    2. 5−(−4)+1= 10
  3. The two solutions of ∣x−c∣=d are −18 and 4. d=
    Show the answer

    11

    1. The solutions sit d on either side of c⁠, so c is their midpoint and d is half the distance between them.
    2. d=(4−(−18))÷2= 11
  4. The sum of the integral solutions of ∣4x+7∣<21 is
    Show the answer

    −15

    1. −21<4x+7<21⁠, so x runs from −7 to 321​⁠, ends not included: integers −6 through 3.
    2. 10 integers centered on −121​⁠: sum =10×(−121​)= −15
