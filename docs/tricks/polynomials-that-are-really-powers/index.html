Polynomials that are really powers

  • Middle school: problems 21–40
  • High school: problems 21–40

Use it when: Evaluating f(x) when f is a perfect square or a row of Pascal's triangle.

How to do it

  1. x2−18x+81=(x−9)2⁠, so f(19)=102=100⁠.
  2. 16x2−40x+25=(4x−5)2⁠: the first and last terms are squares and the middle is twice their product.
  3. Coefficients 1, 3, 3, 1 or 1, 4, 6, 4, 1 (with alternating signs, or all multiplied by the same number) mean (x±1)3 or (x±1)4⁠.
  4. a2+2ab+b2=(a+b)2⁠, and (x−y)(x2+xy+y2)=x3−y3⁠.

Worked example

If f(x)=x2−18x+81⁠, then f(15)=
  1. f(x)=(x−9)2⁠, a perfect square.
  2. (15−9)2=62= 36
Why it worksSpotting the pattern turns a long substitution into one small power.
Watch outCheck the sign in the middle: −40x means (4x−5)2⁠, not (4x+5)2⁠.
