First digits of a decimal

  • Middle school: problems 61–80

Use it when: "The first 4 digits of the decimal for 4533​ are 0.____"

How to do it

  1. Reduce first: 4533​=1511​=0.7333…⁠, so the answer is 7333.
  2. Over 99 (or 9, 11, 33) the two-digit numerator repeats: 9923​=0.2323… → 2323, and 115​=9945​ → 4545.
  3. Over 27, 37 or 111, rewrite over 999: 375​=999135​=0.135135… → 1351.
  4. A 90 or 45 underneath: split off the tenths. 9041​=0.4555… → 4555.
  5. Sevenths cycle through 142857: 73​=0.428571… → 4285.

Worked example

The first four digits after the decimal point of 1510​ are 0.
  1. Reduce first: 1510​=32​⁠. Over 99 the two digits repeat: 32​=9966​⁠.
  2. 32​=0.6666666…⁠, so the first four digits are 6666
Why it worksA repeating block over the same number of 9s is the fraction, so going backward turns a fraction into its digits.
Watch outWrite all four digits, zeros included: 451​=0.0222… → 0222. Don't round the last digit.

Try these

  1. The first four digits after the decimal point of 135​ are 0.
    Show the answer

    3846

    1. Divide 5 by 13.
    2. 135​=0.384615384615…⁠, so the first four digits are 3846
  2. The first four digits after the decimal point of 6635​ are 0.
    Show the answer

    5303

    1. Split off what goes over 10, 100, …, then use the 9s or 11ths pattern.
    2. 6635​=0.5303030…⁠, so the first four digits are 5303
  3. The first four digits after the decimal point of 3732​ are 0.

    1. Over 999 the three digits repeat: 3732​=999864​⁠.
    2. 3732​=0.864864864…⁠, so the first four digits are 8648
  4. The first four digits after the decimal point of 198129​ are 0.
    Show the answer

    6515
