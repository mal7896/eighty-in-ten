Consecutive integers

  • Middle school: problems 41–60
  • High school: problems 21–40

Use it when: "The sum of three consecutive odd integers is 129…".

How to do it

  1. Divide the sum by how many numbers there are.
  2. An odd count gives the middle number. An even count gives the point halfway between the two middle numbers.
  3. Step out to the one they ask for.
  4. From a product: two consecutive numbers multiply to about the square of the smaller, three to about the cube of the middle. Take the root and check.

Worked example

The sum of three consecutive odd integers is 153. The largest is
  1. Middle =153÷3=51⁠.
  2. Largest =51+2= 53
Why it worksConsecutive numbers balance around their average.
Watch outConsecutive odds and evens step by 2.

Try these

  1. Three consecutive integers have a product of 42,840. The middle integer is
    Show the answer

    35

    1. The product is close to the middle number cubed: 342840​ is just under 35.
    2. Check: 34×35×36=42,840⁠. Middle: 35
  2. The sum of four consecutive even integers is 244. The smallest is
    Show the answer

    58

    1. 244÷4=61, the point between the two middle numbers.
    2. Middle pair: 60, 62. Smallest = 58
  3. The sum of four consecutive even integers is 276. The smallest is
    Show the answer

    66

    1. 276÷4=69, the point between the two middle numbers.
    2. Middle pair: 68, 70. Smallest = 66
  4. The sum of five consecutive integers is 290. The largest is
    Show the answer

    60

    1. Middle =290÷5=58⁠.
    2. Largest = 60
