Counting relatively prime numbers

  • Middle school: problems 61–80
  • High school: problems 41–60

Use it when: How many numbers below n share no factor with n⁠?

How to do it

  1. List the distinct primes of n⁠.
  2. Multiply n by (1−p1​) for each one.
  3. 36=22⋅32⁠: 36⋅21​⋅32​=12⁠.
  4. \"Less than or equal to n\" gives the same count as \"less than n\", since n isn't relatively prime to itself.

Worked example

How many positive integers less than 80 are relatively prime to 80?
  1. 80 has prime factors 2 and 5. Take 80 and multiply by (1−21​)(1−51​)⁠.
  2. 80×1/2×4/5= 32
Why it worksThis is Euler's φ⁠. Each prime removes its share of the numbers.
Watch outUse each prime once, no matter its exponent.

Try these

  1. How many positive integers less than or equal to 24 are relatively prime to 24?
    Show the answer

    8

    1. 24 has prime factors 2 and 3. Take 24 and multiply by (1−21​)(1−31​)⁠.
    2. 24×1/2×2/3= 8
  2. How many positive integers less than 60 are relatively prime to 60?
    Show the answer

    16

    1. 60 has prime factors 2, 3 and 5. Take 60 and multiply by (1−21​)(1−31​)(1−51​)⁠.
    2. 60×1/2×2/3×4/5= 16
  3. How many positive integers less than or equal to 45 are relatively prime to 45?
    Show the answer

    24

    1. 45 has prime factors 3 and 5. Take 45 and multiply by (1−31​)(1−51​)⁠.
    2. 45×2/3×4/5= 24
  4. How many positive integers less than 100 are relatively prime to 100?
    Show the answer

    40

    1. 100 has prime factors 2 and 5. Take 100 and multiply by (1−21​)(1−51​)⁠.
    2. 100×1/2×4/5= 40
