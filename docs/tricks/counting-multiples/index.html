Counting multiples

  • Middle school: problems 21–40

Use it when: "How many integers between 20 and 60 are divisible by 7?"

How to do it

  1. Multiples of k from 1 to n⁠: n÷k⁠, dropping the remainder.
  2. Between a and b⁠: the count below b minus the count up to a⁠. Between 20 and 60 by 7: 8−2=6⁠.
  3. Divisible by a or b⁠: add the two counts and take away the multiples of lcm(a,b)⁠, which got counted twice. "Neither": take that from the total.

Worked example

How many integers between 38 and 111 are divisible by 11?
  1. Multiples of 11 below 111:110÷11→10 (drop the remainder). Multiples up to 38:38÷11→3⁠.
  2. 10−3= 7
Why it worksEvery kth number is a multiple of k⁠.
Watch out"Between" leaves out both ends. "From 1 to n" and "less than or equal to n" include n⁠; "less than n" doesn't.

Try these

  1. How many integers between 56 and 122 are divisible by 7?
    Show the answer

    9

    1. Multiples of 7 below 122:121÷7→17 (drop the remainder). Multiples up to 56:56÷7→8⁠.
    2. 17−8= 9
  2. How many integers from 1 to 78 are divisible by neither 8 nor 11?
    Show the answer

    62

    1. Up to 78: multiples of 8: 9; of 11: 7; of both (multiples of 88): 0.
    2. Divisible by one or the other: 9+7−0=16⁠. Neither: 78−16= 62
  3. How many positive integers less than 293 are divisible by 7 or 8?
    Show the answer

    72

    1. Up to 292: multiples of 7: 41; of 8: 36; of both (multiples of 56): 5.
    2. 41+36−5= 72
  4. How many positive terms does the sequence 117, 114, 111, … have?
    Show the answer

    39

    1. The terms are 117−3k for k=0,1,2,…⁠, and stay positive while 3k<117⁠.
    2. k can be 0 through 38: 39 terms
