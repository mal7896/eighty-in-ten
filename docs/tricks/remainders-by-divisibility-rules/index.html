Remainders by divisibility rules

  • Middle school: problems 1–20
  • High school: problems 1–20

Use it when: A big number divided by 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 11 or 12.

How to do it

  1. ÷3 or ÷9⁠: add the digits.
  2. ÷4⁠: last two digits. ÷8⁠: last three digits.
  3. ÷11⁠: alternating sum of the digits, starting from the ones digit.
  4. ÷6 or ÷12⁠: find the remainder for 2 (or 4) and for 3, then the number that fits both.

Worked example

5802÷8 has a remainder of
  1. Only the last 3 digits matter for 8: 802.
  2. 802÷8 leaves 2
Why it works10≡1(mod9)⁠, so the digits just add. 10≡−1(mod11)⁠, so they alternate.
Watch outFor 11, a negative alternating sum needs 11s added until it lands between 0 and 10.

Try these

  1. 8444÷3 has a remainder of
    Show the answer

    2

    1. Add the digits: 8+4+4+4=20⁠.
    2. 20÷3 leaves 2
  2. 947,850÷6 has a remainder of
    Show the answer

    0

    1. Split 6=2×3⁠. Remainder by 2: 0. Remainder by 3 (digit sum): 0.
    2. The number 0–5 with both of those remainders is 0
  3. 9047÷4 has a remainder of
    Show the answer

    3

    1. Only the last 2 digits matter for 4: 47.
    2. 47÷4 leaves 3
  4. 951,591÷7 has a remainder of
    Show the answer

    4

    1. 7 divides 1001, so split into groups of three from the right and alternate signs: 591−951=−360⁠.
    2. −360÷7 leaves 4
