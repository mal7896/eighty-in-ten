GCD and LCM

  • Middle school: problems 21–40
  • High school: problems 1–20

Use it when: Greatest common divisor or least common multiple.

How to do it

  1. GCD: the biggest factor both share.
  2. LCM of two numbers: gcda×b​⁠.
  3. And always: gcd×lcm=a×b⁠.
  4. lcm(52,78)=52×​⁠: the blank is 78÷gcd=78÷26=3⁠.

Worked example

The GCD of 36 and 60 is
  1. 36=12×3,60=12×5, and 3, 5 share nothing.
  2. GCD = 12
Why it worksEvery prime factor lands once in the GCD and once in the LCM.
Watch outFor three numbers, take the highest power of each prime.

Try these

  1. The sum of the LCM and the GCD of 24 and 18 is
    Show the answer

    78

    1. GCD(24,18)=6⁠. LCM =24×18÷6=72⁠.
    2. 72+6= 78
  2. The sum of the LCM and the GCD of 28 and 70 is
    Show the answer

    154

    1. GCD(28,70)=14⁠. LCM =28×70÷14=140⁠.
    2. 140+14= 154
  3. The LCM of 63 and 81 is
    Show the answer

    567

    1. GCD is 9. LCM = a × b ÷ GCD =63×81÷9⁠.
    2. =63×9= 567
  4. The LCM of 14 and 49 is 14×
    Show the answer

    7

    1. The LCM is 14 times whatever 49 has that 14 doesn't: 49÷ GCD(14,49)=49÷7⁠.
    2. = 7
