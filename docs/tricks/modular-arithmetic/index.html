Modular arithmetic

  • High school: problems 61–80

Use it when: Solving ax≡b(modm)⁠, or finding a number with given remainders.

How to do it

  1. For ax≡b⁠: add copies of m to b until a divides it.
  2. For two remainders: list numbers with one remainder, check the other.
  3. With a range, like 3x−4≡5(mod9) for 4≤x≤8⁠: move the constant (3x≡9⁠), find one solution (x=3⁠), then keep adding m÷gcd(a,m) until you land in the range: 3,6,9 → 6.
  4. Big powers when the modulus is a prime p⁠: ap−1≡1 (Fermat), so divide the exponent by p−1 and keep the remainder. For 3100 and 7: 100=6⋅16+4⁠, so 34=81≡4⁠.

Worked example

Find x,0≤x≤8, if 5x≡7 (mod 9)⁠. x=
  1. Try x=0,1,2,… or add 9s to 7 until 5 divides it.
  2. 5×5=25, and 25≡7 (mod 9)⁠. x= 5
Why it worksNumbers that differ by a multiple of m leave the same remainder when divided by m⁠.
Watch outThe answer should be between 0 and m−1 unless the problem says otherwise.

    2. Solutions are 1 plus multiples of 6. The one from 0 to 5 is 1
