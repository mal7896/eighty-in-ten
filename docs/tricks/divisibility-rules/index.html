Divisibility rules

  • Middle school: problems 21–40
  • High school: problems 21–40

Use it when: Finding the missing digit k so a number is divisible by 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 11, 12, 15 or 18.

How to do it

  1. 3 or 9: the digit sum is divisible by 3 or 9.
  2. 4: the last two digits make a multiple of 4. 8: the last three digits make a multiple of 8.
  3. 11: the alternating sum of the digits (+−+−⁠) is a multiple of 11, and 0 counts.
  4. 6, 12, 15, 18: check both parts. 6 is 2 and 3, 12 is 3 and 4, 15 is 3 and 5, 18 is 2 and 9.

Worked example

The 4-digit number 743k is divisible by 6. k=
  1. Divisible by 6 means: even and digit sum divisible by 3.
  2. Only one digit works: k= 4
Why it works10 leaves remainder 1 when divided by 3 or 9, and remainder −1 when divided by 11. So each digit counts once, or with alternating signs for 11.
Watch outUsually only one nonzero digit works. If two seem to, recheck the second condition.
