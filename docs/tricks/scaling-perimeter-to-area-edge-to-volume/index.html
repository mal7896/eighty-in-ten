Scaling: perimeter to area, edge to volume

  • Middle school: problems 61–80
  • High school: problems 41–60

Use it when: "The radius of a circle increases by 20%. The area increases by ___%." "The perimeter of a square goes from 16 to 28; the area increases by ___."

How to do it

  1. Lengths (side, radius, perimeter, circumference) scale by k⁠, areas by k2⁠, volumes by k3⁠.
  2. For a percent: 1.22=1.44 → 44% more. 0.92=0.81 → 19% less. 1.13=1.331 → 33.1% more.
  3. For a change in area, find the two sides and use a difference of squares: perimeters 16 and 28 → sides 4 and 7 → (7−4)(7+4)=33⁠.
  4. Circles: circumference cπ means radius 2c​⁠. From 10π to 14π⁠: (72−52)π=24π⁠.

Worked example

The circumference of a circle increases from 16π to 24π⁠. The area increases by kπ⁠. k=
  1. Radii: 8 and 12.
  2. Change in area: (122−82)π=(12−8)(12+8)π⁠, so k= 80
Why it worksArea is length times length, so it picks up the scale factor twice. Volume picks it up three times.
Watch outA 20% increase in side is not a 40% increase in area. Square the factor, then subtract 1.

Try these

  1. When the perimeter of a square increases from 8 to 32, the increase in area is
    Show the answer

    60

    1. Sides: 8÷4=2 and 32÷4=8⁠.
    2. Difference of squares: (8−2)(8+2)= 60
  2. If each edge of a cube decreases by 10%, the volume decreases by %
    Show the answer

    27.1

    1. Lengths scale by 0.9, so the volume scales by 0.93=0.729⁠.
    2. That's a 27.1% decrease
  3. If each edge of a cube increases by 20%, the volume increases by %
    Show the answer

    72.8

    1. Lengths scale by 1.2, so the volume scales by 1.23=1.728⁠.
    2. That's a 72.8% increase
  4. If each edge of a cube increases by 100%, the volume increases by %
    Show the answer

    700

    1. Lengths scale by 2, so the volume scales by 23=8⁠.
    2. That's a 700% increase
