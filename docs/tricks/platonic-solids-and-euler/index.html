Platonic solids and Euler

  • Middle school: problems 41–60
  • High school: problems 41–60

Use it when: Faces, vertices and edges of the regular solids.

How to do it

  1. Tetrahedron 4 F, 4 V, 6 E. Cube 6, 8, 12. Octahedron 8, 6, 12. Dodecahedron 12, 20, 30. Icosahedron 20, 12, 30.
  2. Euler: F+V−E=2⁠.

Worked example

A convex polyhedron has 7 faces and 19 vertices. How many edges does it have?
  1. Euler: F + V − E =2⁠.
  2. E =7+19−2= 24
Why it worksThe cube and octahedron swap faces and vertices. So do the dodecahedron and icosahedron.
Watch outA hexahedron is a cube.

Try these

  1. How many faces does a cube have?
    Show the answer

    6

    1. Cube: 6 faces, 8 vertices, 12 edges.
    2. Answer: 6
  2. How many edges does a regular tetrahedron have?
    Show the answer

    6

    1. Tetrahedron: 4 faces, 4 vertices, 6 edges.
    2. Answer: 6
  3. How many vertices does a cube have?
    Show the answer

    8

    1. Cube: 6 faces, 8 vertices, 12 edges.
    2. Answer: 8
  4. A convex polyhedron has 16 faces and 13 vertices. How many edges does it have?
    Show the answer

    27

    1. Euler: F + V − E =2⁠.
    2. E =16+13−2= 27
