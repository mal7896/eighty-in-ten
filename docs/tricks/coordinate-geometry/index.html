Coordinate geometry

  • Middle school: problems 41–60
  • High school: problems 41–60

Use it when: Distance, midpoint, slope, intercepts and distance to a line.

How to do it

  1. Distance: look for a triple in the x and y changes.
  2. Slope of Ax+By=C is −BA​⁠, and the y−intercept is BC​⁠.
  3. From a point to Ax+By+C=0⁠: A2+B2​∣Ax0​+By0​+C∣​⁠.
  4. Circle through a point: r2 is the squared distance from the center, so the area is πr2 with no square roots.
  5. Missing endpoint from a midpoint: far end =2× midpoint − near end.
  6. Intercepts (a,0) and (0,b)⁠: slope −ab​⁠. Perpendicular to Ax+By=C⁠: slope AB​⁠.
  7. Parallel lines have equal slopes; perpendicular slopes multiply to −1⁠. A perpendicular bisector has the negative reciprocal of the segment's slope.

Worked example

(−2, 2) is the midpoint of the segment from (5, 3) to (x,y)⁠. Find x−y⁠.
  1. Each coordinate of the far end is twice the midpoint minus the near end: x=2(−2)−(5)=−9,y=2(2)−(3)=1⁠.
  2. x−y= −10
Why it worksDistance is the Pythagorean theorem.
Watch outKeep the minus sign in −BA​⁠.
