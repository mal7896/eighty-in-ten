Patterns with 9s and 8s

  • Middle school: problems 1–20
  • High school: problems 1–20

Use it when: Products like 98765×9+3 or 9×1234+5 that land on a row of repeated digits.

How to do it

  1. 9×12+3=111⁠, 9×123+4=1111⁠, 9×1234+5=11111⁠: one more 1 than the number has digits.
  2. 9×9+7=88⁠, 98×9+6=888⁠, 987×9+5=8888⁠: one more 8 than the number has digits.
  3. 9×321−1=2888⁠, 9×4321−1=38888⁠: one less than the first digit, then a row of 8s.
  4. 12×8+2=98⁠, 123×8+3=987⁠: the digits count down from 9.
  5. 112=121⁠, 1112=12321⁠: a row of 1s squared counts up and back down (this works up to nine 1s).

Worked example

9×21−1=
  1. 9×21−1=188,9×321−1=2888,9×4321−1=38888⁠: one less than the first digit, then a row of 8s.
  2. 1, then 2 eights: 188
Why it worksEach pattern grows one digit at a time, and you can always check the last digit: 98765×9 ends in 5, and 5+3=8⁠.
Watch outThese only work with the exact digit runs shown. 9×1243+5 is 11192, not 11111.
