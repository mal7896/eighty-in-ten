Four in a row, plus one

  • Middle school: problems 41–60
  • High school: problems 41–60

Use it when: n(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)+1​⁠.

How to do it

  1. Multiply the first and last numbers and add 1.
  2. 7⋅8⋅9⋅10+1​=7⋅10+1=71⁠.

Worked example

9×10×11×12+1​=
  1. Four consecutive numbers multiplied, plus 1, is always a perfect square: the square of (first × last +1)⁠.
  2. 9×12+1= 109
Why it worksn(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)+1=(n2+3n+1)2⁠.
Watch outWithout the square root, the answer is that number squared.
