Prices, discounts, interest

  • Middle school: problems 21–40
  • High school: problems 1–20

Use it when: Unit prices, sale prices, tips and simple interest.

How to do it

  1. Unit price: find one, then scale.
  2. p% off means you pay (100−p)%⁠.
  3. Simple interest: principal × rate × time.
  4. Raises and cuts: p% more means multiply by 1+100p​⁠. Two raises in a row multiply. They don't add.
  5. Sales tax: price × rate. 6.5% of 60 dollars is 3.90.
  6. Coins: count in nickels. A dime is 2 nickels and a quarter is 5. Interest for months: use months ÷12 years.

Worked example

6 gallons at $3.47 a gallon costs $
  1. Round the price up to $4.00 and take back the extra: 6×53=318 cents.
  2. 6×$4.00−$3.18=$20.82
Why it worksThe discount and the price you pay always add to 100%.
Watch outMoney answers are decimals: 42.50, not 4221​⁠.

Try these

  1. The simple interest on $1000 at 4% a year for 9 months is $
    Show the answer

    30.00

    1. Interest = principal × rate × time (in years: 9/12)⁠.
    2. 1000×4/100×9/12=$30.00
  2. A weekly salary of $780 is decreased 5%. The new salary is $
    Show the answer

    741.00

    1. 5% of 780=39⁠.
    2. 780−39=$741.00
  3. A $60 jacket is 25% off. The sale price is $
    Show the answer

    45.00

    1. You pay 75% of the price.
    2. 75% of 60=$45
  4. 12 gallons at $3.32 a gallon costs $
    Show the answer

    39.84

    1. Round the price up to $4.00 and take back the extra: 12×68=816 cents.
    2. 12×$4.00−$8.16=$39.84
