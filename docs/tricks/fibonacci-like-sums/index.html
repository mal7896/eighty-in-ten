Fibonacci-like sums

  • Middle school: problems 61–80
  • High school: problems 41–60

Use it when: Adding up a sequence where each term is the sum of the two before it.

How to do it

  1. Exactly ten terms: the sum is 11 times the 7th term. 2+7+9+16+25+41+66+107+173+280=11×66=726⁠.
  2. Any number of terms: the sum of the first n terms is the term two places later minus the 2nd term.
  3. If you're told the total of ten terms, the 7th term is that total ÷11⁠.

Worked example

1+7+8+15+23+⋯+160=
  1. In a Fibonacci-like sequence, the sum of the first n terms is the term two places later minus the 2nd term.
  2. The terms continue 99, 160, 259, 419. The term after 160 is 259, then 419. 419−7= 412
Why it worksWrite each term using the first two, a and b⁠. The first ten add to 55a+88b⁠, and the 7th term is 5a+8b⁠, exactly an eleventh of it.
Watch outThe 11 trick needs exactly ten terms. For any other count, use the "two places later" rule.

Try these

  1. The sum of the first 10 terms of a,b,a+b,a+2b,2a+3b,… is 429. The 7th term is
    Show the answer

    39

    1. The sum of the first ten terms is always 11 times the 7th term.
    2. 429÷11= 39

    Show the answer

    88

    1. The sum of the first ten terms is always 11 times the 7th term.
    2. 968÷11= 88
  3. 9+4+13+17+30+⋯+526=
    Show the answer

    1373

    1. In a Fibonacci-like sequence, the sum of the first n terms is the term two places later minus the 2nd term.
    2. The terms continue 325, 526, 851, 1377. The term after 526 is 851, then 1377. 1377−4= 1373
  4. 8+7+15+22+37+59+96+155+251+406=
    Show the answer

    1056

    1. Each term is the sum of the two before it, and there are 10 terms. The sum of 10 such terms is 11 times the 7th term.
    2. 11×96= 1056
